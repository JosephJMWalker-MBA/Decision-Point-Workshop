# AI Architecture Discovery Challenge — Governance v0.1

## Governance objective

The competition should be difficult for any one founder, laboratory, sponsor, ideology, or incumbent architecture to capture.

Institutional participation is useful because different organizations see different failure modes. It should add scrutiny without turning the competition into a collection of logos or a popularity vote.

## Standing Evaluation Council

Decision Point Initiative may invite relevant organizations to nominate **one qualified representative** to a standing evaluation council.

Potential constituencies include:

- AI laboratories and technical research organizations;
- AI governance and safety organizations;
- universities and independent researchers;
- standards, assurance, security, or reproducibility experts;
- other institutions with directly relevant expertise.

Each participating organization may nominate one representative. Nomination does not guarantee that every representative scores every submission; the standing council is the broader pool from which balanced competition panels can be formed.

Participation does not imply endorsement of Decision Point Initiative, the competition, or any architecture.

## Council responsibilities

The council may help review and improve:

- competition rules;
- evaluation criteria;
- benchmark legitimacy;
- hard gates;
- safety boundaries;
- baseline selection;
- challenge-test proposals;
- disclosure rules;
- reproducibility requirements;
- conflict-of-interest procedures.

Where useful, council members and participating organizations may propose challenge criteria before submissions are frozen.

For example, a governance organization might propose an intervention test for a corrigibility claim, while a technical laboratory might propose a scaling, adaptation, or generalization test.

The goal is to let institutions contribute intellectual ownership to the **quality of the measurement** without giving them control over the outcome.

## Competition panels

Final scoring should be performed by smaller rotating panels drawn from the standing council and, where necessary, additional independent evaluators.

Panels should be balanced across at least three functions:

1. **technical evaluation** — whether the architecture works and the engineering claims are credible;
2. **governance / safety evaluation** — whether control, auditability, failure, and intervention claims survive scrutiny;
3. **reproducibility / scientific evaluation** — whether evidence, baselines, methods, and reported results can be independently checked.

No constituency should dominate merely because more organizations from that constituency accepted an invitation.

## Mandatory recusal

A judge must recuse from evaluating a submission when the judge or their organization:

- submitted or materially contributed to the architecture;
- employs or directly supervises a material contributor;
- funds the relevant team in a way that creates a meaningful conflict;
- has a close collaboration or financial relationship that reasonably calls independence into question;
- has another material conflict that should be disclosed.

Recusals and material conflicts should be recorded in the public evaluation record whenever safe and lawful to disclose.

The existence of contributors from the same organization as a council member does not automatically exclude those contributors from participation; it triggers recusal where appropriate.

## Pre-registration and rule changes

Evaluation criteria, weights, major tests, resource limits, and scoring procedures should be frozen before final submissions are evaluated.

If a rule or test must change after submissions are visible, the competition should document:

- what changed;
- why it changed;
- who approved the change;
- which submissions are affected;
- whether retesting is required.

Judges should not be able to quietly change the standard after seeing which architecture performs well.

## Evidence before preference

The intended norm is:

> No architecture wins because judges like it. It wins recognition because its published claims survive the tests the evaluation process agreed upon.

Judgment remains necessary where evidence is incomplete, criteria conflict, or scientific uncertainty cannot be reduced to a score. Written rationale should make that judgment visible.

## Founder and organizer firewall

Decision Point Initiative convenes the process and Decision Point Workshop hosts the public work.

The organizer may contribute ideas, tests, or architectures, but an organizer-affiliated architecture receives no privileged evaluation status.

If an organizer or founder materially contributes to a submission:

- that role must be disclosed;
- the organizer must not control its scoring panel;
- conflicted judges must recuse;
- independent reproduction should receive extra emphasis;
- final rationale should be written by non-conflicted evaluators.

A founder-affiliated architecture losing under a credible process is not a failure of the Workshop. It is evidence that the process can generate information the founder did not control.

## Sponsors and funding

Sponsorship may support evaluation, participant resources, prizes, infrastructure, or event costs, but it must not purchase:

- eligibility;
- favorable judging;
- extra votes;
- guaranteed recognition;
- suppression of unfavorable findings;
- ownership of the competition's conclusions.

Financial relationships relevant to judging should be disclosed.

## Recognition governance

Awards should follow evidence rather than create prestige in advance.

No participant should be required to pay to receive an earned award. Categories may remain unawarded if the evidence does not justify recognition.

Written award rationales should identify the contribution or evidence being recognized so that the public record matters more than the trophy.

## Controlled findings

Some adversarial findings may be dangerous to publish in operational detail.

The competition should distinguish between:

- public evidence sufficient to support a conclusion;
- controlled technical details shared only with authorized evaluators or affected teams;
- findings whose release should be delayed or withheld for safety reasons.

Withholding operational exploit detail should not erase the contributor's credit or the fact that a claim failed.

## Governance is itself testable

This document is a v0.1 proposal, not a constitutional endpoint.

Contributors are explicitly invited to identify capture risks, conflicts, perverse incentives, missing constituencies, or simpler governance arrangements that would make the process more trustworthy.
