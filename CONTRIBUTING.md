# Contributing to Decision Point Workshop

Decision Point Workshop is meant to be participated in.

You do not need a title, institutional affiliation, invitation, or agreement with the founder to make a useful contribution. The standard is whether the contribution helps people reason more clearly about a consequential decision point.

## Ways to contribute

### Claim or propose a work packet

Use the [Work Packet issue template](https://github.com/JosephJMWalker-MBA/Decision-Point-Workshop/issues/new?template=work-packet.md) when you want to take on a bounded task or propose one that is missing.

### Submit a contribution

Use the [Contribution issue template](https://github.com/JosephJMWalker-MBA/Decision-Point-Workshop/issues/new?template=contribution.md) for a finding, analysis, artifact, prior-art result, experiment, comparison, or synthesis.

### Challenge a claim

Use the [Challenge / Falsification issue template](https://github.com/JosephJMWalker-MBA/Decision-Point-Workshop/issues/new?template=challenge.md) to identify a weak assumption, missing source, hidden tradeoff, contradictory result, better explanation, or reason a workshop premise should be abandoned.

A good challenge should make the work more accurate even when it makes the current proposal less persuasive.

### Propose a decision point

Use the [Decision Point proposal template](https://github.com/JosephJMWalker-MBA/Decision-Point-Workshop/issues/new?template=decision-point.md) to surface another consequential choice that may deserve a public workshop.

A useful proposal should answer:

1. What choice is being made or allowed to become inevitable?
2. Who is affected?
3. What alternatives are currently underexplored?
4. What evidence would help distinguish among them?
5. Why does the decision need attention now?

### Submit durable work

Open a pull request for workshop documents, source material, structured analyses, code, experiments, syntheses, or other artifacts that should live in version history.

## Contribution posture

DPI values contributions that:

- distinguish evidence from interpretation;
- preserve uncertainty and provenance;
- represent competing explanations fairly;
- make falsification possible;
- identify simpler alternatives;
- avoid overstating what research or prototypes establish;
- preserve human accountability for consequential decisions;
- improve the work rather than merely reinforce the current thesis.

## AI-assisted work

AI use is welcome. When an AI system materially contributed to a result, identify the model or tool when practical and distinguish what the human contributor inspected, reasoned through, tested, observed, or verified.

The goal is not to stigmatize AI assistance. It is to preserve enough provenance that another contributor can understand how the result was produced.

## Current priority

The current priority is **DP-001 — National Competition for Governable AI Architectures**.

Especially useful contributions include:

- prior art on AI safety architecture competitions, challenge programs, verification, or red teaming;
- arguments that the competition itself would create unacceptable risk;
- candidate governance properties that can actually be measured;
- candidate architectures substantially different from MASI or Governed Intelligence Ecology;
- adversarial evaluation designs;
- international coordination models;
- challenge-design experience from government, academia, standards bodies, or technical competitions;
- ways to prevent a safety competition from accidentally becoming a capability accelerator.

## What contribution does not require

Contributing does not require endorsing every DPI project, joining a political program, adopting a shared ideology, or accepting the founder's preferred solution.

Disagreement can be a contribution.
